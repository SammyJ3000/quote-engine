# Rates and price research — Northwest England
Researched 2026-08-20. Region: Northwest England (Greater Manchester,
Merseyside, Lancashire, Cheshire). All figures GBP, ex VAT, labour-only,
8-hour day.

## Method

Regional figures are preferred over national ones throughout. Where a source
gave only a national figure and a stated regional adjustment, the adjustment
was applied and the result is labelled "NW-adjusted" — it is never averaged
with a directly-regional figure without saying so. Where sources disagreed the
full range is recorded and the midpoint of the per-source midpoints was taken,
then rounded to the nearest £5.

Sources fall into three families, which disagree systematically:

- **Marketplace databases** (HaMuch) — real quoted jobs, regional, but skewed
  low because they are dominated by small homeowner repair work.
- **Trade day-rate guides** (Sleepless Tradesman, tradedayrates.co.uk) — skewed
  high; they quote what a tradesperson *should* charge, not what is achieved.
- **Estimating price books** (Concretemath, Renocalc, CostEstimator) — mid,
  and closest to what a builder actually pays a subcontractor.

All three families were used for every trade so no single bias dominates.

## Part B — Material list prices

### How to read these

The figures in `catalogue/manual_prices.yaml` are **list prices, ex VAT**. The
discount matrix in `clients/demo-nw/discounts.yaml` then takes the trade
discount off them to reach the builder's buying price. Nothing here is
pre-discounted.

UK retail prices are almost always displayed inc VAT. Every such figure was
divided by 1.2; both numbers appear below. Getting this wrong in either
direction moves the whole quote by 20%, so it is recorded explicitly per line.

### Modelling caveat worth flagging to the owner

The discount matrix (timber 38%, sanitaryware 35%, heavy-side 32%) is
calibrated for **builders' merchant list prices**, where those discounts are
real. Several prices above come from Screwfix and Wickes, whose shelf prices
are already keen and do not carry a further 38% trade discount. Where that
applies the resulting buying price is optimistic — CLS timber lands at £1.99/
length and a downlight at £5.37. The totals still sense-check (see Part D), but
if the owner wants these tighter the fix is to re-source the timber and
heavy-side lines from Travis Perkins or Huws Gray list prices rather than to
change the discount matrix.

## Part C — Two corrections

### C1: plastering allowance

Was `0.5 + area * 0.06` — 16.7 m²/day, roughly half what a plasterer achieves.

Researched skim rates:

- https://argocontractors.co.uk/how-much-can-a-plasterer-do-in-a-day/ —
  "a decent plasterer can typically skim around 25 m² per day"
- https://ukbusinessguide.wixsite.com/unitelseo/post/how-many-square-metres-can-a-plasterer-do-in-a-day —
  20–30 m²/day for a skilled plasterer; 20–40 m²/day experienced
- https://www.bestbuilders.co.uk/costs/plastering-cost-uk — skim over sound
  plaster £14–£22/m², consistent with ~25 m²/day at a £230–£280 day rate

**Set to `0.5 + area * 0.035`** = 28.6 m²/day for the variable part, plus half
a day fixed for making good, mixing and setting up. 0.035 sits in the middle of
the 25–35 m²/day band the brief specified and at the upper-middle of the 20–30
band the sources give — justified because the fixed half-day already absorbs
the set-up and detail work that drags the all-in figure down. On the large
full-move case this comes out at 1.51 days for 28.96 m², i.e. ~19 m²/day
all-in, which is an honest bathroom number.

Diagnosis, as requested — the two allowances carrying it:

1. **`floor_lvt`: `0.5 + floor_area_m2 / 8`.** On the large full-move case this

   top double-counts set-up. This is the single most generous allowance in the
   recipe and the main driver of large-fullmove's 61.0%.
2. **`decorating`: `0.5 + area * 0.05`.** 20 m²/day for mist plus two coats is

change it. That job is 9.1 man-days on £1,003 of budget materials; the fixed
minima (first fix 1.0d, second fix 1.0d, second-fix electrical 0.5d, final fix
0.5d) do not shrink with the room, while budget sanitaryware and vinyl are
genuinely cheap. Small budget bathrooms really are labour-heavy. Forcing that
one under 60% would mean lying about the man-days.

Neither change was made: both allowances are outside the C1/C2 scope this task
was given. Flagged for the owner to decide.

### E1 — `tanking-kit` coverage exceeded the product it was priced from

`materials.yaml` declared `per_unit: 5.0`, but the Screwfix Mapei Shower
Waterproofing Kit the £49.99 came from states "covers up to 4m²". A 4.5 m²
walk-in wet area was therefore buying **one** kit where two are needed — a
straight under-buy on the flagship shower type.

Screwfix's wet-room and shower-waterproofing categories were re-checked
(2026-08-21) for a genuine ≥5 m² **kit**: there is none. The only larger option
is Mapei AquaDefense 7.5kg at £54.99, which is a membrane on its own — no
primer, no jointing tape — so it is not the same product class as this SKU.

**Fixed by dropping `per_unit` to 4.0** to match the product actually priced.
Price unchanged at £41.66 ex (£49.99 inc). A walk-in now takes 2 kits
(4.5 m² → ceil(4.5/4)), an enclosure still takes 1 (3.5 m²).

### E2 — `wetroom_former_tiling` charged tiler days with no tiles behind them

The task carried 0.5 tiler-days and no materials, on the grounds that `tanking`
and `floor_tiling` already carried them. That reasoning fails on
`bathroom-large-fullmove`: walk-in **and** `tiling_extent: floor-only` **and**
`floor_type: lvt` means both `wall_tiling` and `floor_tiling` evaluate `when`
to false, so the job paid £110 of tiler time to tile into a former with tiles
nobody bought.

Two fixes were offered: give the task its own tile quantity, or gate it behind
floor tiling. **Chose the first.** A walk-in former has to be tiled into
whatever the rest of the floor is — that is what makes the drain and the fall
work — so the labour is real on an LVT job and gating it off would under-price
a real walk-in. Gating would also make the *labour* conditional on a *finishes*
choice, which is the wrong coupling.

Added to the task:

- `floor-tile-mid`, 1.75 m², waste 0.15 — a 1200×900 former plus its immediate
  surround. Waste is 15% rather than the floor's 12% because every cut follows
  the fall to the drain.
- `adhesive-flexible-20kg` and `grout-5kg`, both `by_coverage` on the same
  1.75 m².

Effect: +£86.47 of material on both walk-in golden cases. On a tiled-floor
walk-in this overlaps the `floor_tiling` area by 1.75 m²; that is accepted and
is not padding — radial cuts into a fall generate real offcuts that a flat 12%
on a rectangle does not cover. Each tiling task already ceils its own adhesive
and grout bags independently, so this is consistent with the rest of the recipe.
